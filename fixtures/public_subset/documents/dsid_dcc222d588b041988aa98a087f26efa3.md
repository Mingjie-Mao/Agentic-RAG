SLA gradient autoscaler and burst fidelity mapper

Context and motivation: Dedicated fleets see a lot of short-lived bursty demand (new product launches, cron waves) where absolute utilization is a poor predictor of imminent SLO violations. Current autoscalers use smoothed utilization or percentile thresholds and either scale too late or thrash in the face of noise. This PR implements two complementary controls: (1) SLA gradient autoscaler — a controller that estimates the time-derivative of SLO breach probability (the 'SLA gradient') from per-tenant latency percentiles and request rate change, and uses that as the primary signal for scale-up/scale-down decisions; (2) Burst fidelity mapper — a transient routing policy that maps short-lived surge traffic to lower-fidelity model variants or reduced token budgets to preserve throughput and reduce SLO pressure without immediate capacity expansion.

Design summary: SLA gradient estimator computes a short-window slope of P(latency > target) for the 95th and 99th percentiles using exponential smoothing and outlier-resistant derivative estimation. The autoscaler uses a PID-like control with asymmetric gains (aggressive on positive gradient, conservative on negative) and a time-decay headroom budget to avoid over-provisioning. The burst fidelity mapper sits in the request router; it evaluates burst eligibility based on (recent gradient magnitude, request age, tenant burst credits) and applies a fidelity policy (model variant, token cap, or response truncation). We include safety fallbacks: if fidelity routing increases error-rate metrics beyond a configured threshold, requests are routed back to full-fidelity or capacity is proactively expanded.

Why this is different: Instead of only reacting to utilization, the controller anticipates SLO violations by tracking how quickly the risk is changing. The fidelity mapper provides a fast, low-cost knob to buy time for scale decisions and preserve customer experience.

Performance notes from local bench + canary (us-west-2, dedicated small-pool):
- 95p tail latency breaches during synthetic burst reduced from 2.8% to 0.7% (4x reduction) when autoscaler + fidelity mapper active
- Median cost (GPU-hours * price) during ramp increased 1.2% due to earlier small-scale pre-warms but was offset by fidelity routing reducing burst duration
- Scale-up decision latency improved (median detection-to-provision path) by ~18% because gradient signal allowed earlier pre-provisioning

Rollout plan: initial canary on a subset of dedicated pools (us-west-2) with fidelity mapper enabled for low-risk tenants. Use feature flag (ff:sla_gradient_autoscaler) tied to tenant groups. Monitor fidelity_reversion_count and error-rate. If fidelity routing causes >0.5% absolute increase in task-level error-rate for >30m, automatically pause fidelity mapper.
