introduce heatmap-driven warmup orchestrator and quant-sampler

Motivation: Several customers and internal cost audits showed recurring spikes in per-token cost for bursty, low-latency workloads caused by cold KV/embedding caches and suboptimal batch templates. Static prefill counts are brittle across tenants and sequence-length distributions. This PR adds a heatmap-driven warmup orchestrator that computes tenant-specific warmup plans and a quant-sampler that safely evaluates compact quant profiles against representative microtraces to choose cost-vs-quality candidates.

What this PR does (single-line sections concatenated per repo norms): implements heatmap aggregation from sampling telemetry, a warmup scheduler that issues controlled warmup traffic to inference runners, a lightweight quant-sampler that replays synthetic microtraces against candidate quant settings and reports estimated token-cost delta and a quality proxy score, a new CLI subcommand (redwood cost-orch simulate/warmup) and a small dashboard panel in the Console for warmup heatmaps and quant-sampler summaries. Adds e2e gating for the warmup flow and unit/e2e tests for the quant-sampler to detect regressions.

Backward compatibility and safety: warmup is opt-in per-tenant via new tenant config (defaults off). The orchestrator uses a conservative quota and a safety fallback that disables warmup if any late-stage runner reports >2x observed latency or >0.5% error increase during the trial window. Quant-sampler never auto-promotes a profile; it only writes candidate ranking data to the tenant's recommended-profiles store for manual review or an opt-in rollout.

Migration and rollout: config is guarded behind feature flag cost_orch_heatmap_v1. Migration steps included in docs/ops/cost-orch/README.md. Merged as a feature-flagged rollout; mobile and web clients unaffected.

Why this is different from previous prefill work: instead of static prefill heuristics, the orchestrator builds a heatmap of cold vs hot key-space per-tenant and shapes warmup traffic to cover the highest-impact KV regions first. The quant-sampler ties the warmup-shaped microtraces to quant evaluation to produce more accurate unit-econ estimates for candidate profiles.

Testing notes: Ran load smoke in perf-lab with 10 tenants synthetic traffic (reflecting typical PLG spike) — warmup reduced observed per-token cost by ~18% in steady-state for those tenants after warmup completion (simulated); end-to-end latency increase during warmup stayed under 6ms p95 and error rates were within 0.2% of baseline. Quant-sampler experiments included a holdout eval set; results written to artifacts/cost-orch/quant-sampler/2026-01-28/
